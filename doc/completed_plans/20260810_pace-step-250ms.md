# Plan: Pace Step to 250 ms

## Summary

Step the serial Spotify request lane's minimum start gap from 1,000 ms to
250 ms under a new recorded policy stamp, `serial-250ms-v1`. Everything
else about the lane -- one in-flight request, write ordering, the 429
cooldown and single bounded retry, cancellation -- stays exactly as it is.
The step is justified by recorded telemetry and remains attributable in
telemetry, so the next decision (a further step or a rollback) is again
made from evidence.

## Problem

The lane's 1,000 ms gap was set deliberately conservative and explicitly
"unconfigurable until the telemetry evidence justifies a different pace".
That evidence now exists. As of 2026-08-10 the telemetry store holds 203
scored requests under `serial-1000ms-v1`:

- 198 clean requests across playlist listing, metadata, and item reads and
  writes, including four complete 4,549-track shuffles of 47 requests each
  that sustained the policy ceiling -- a rolling window of 30 requests per
  30 seconds -- start to finish with zero refusals. The limiter has never
  engaged at this pace on these endpoints.
- The only refusals ever recorded are five 429s on the liked-tracks
  endpoint at rolling-window counts of 1 to 7 -- one was the first request
  of its window -- with no `Retry-After`, and the post-cooldown retry
  failed twice. Those are pressure-independent quota refusals; pacing
  demonstrably neither causes nor cures them.

Meanwhile the gap is the entire user-visible cost of a shuffle: a
4,549-track cache-hit shuffle issues 47 requests and takes about 47
seconds, matching the gap arithmetic exactly. The evidence shows 1 req/s
is comfortably below any pressure limit but cannot certify a specific
faster number, so the change must be a bounded step that telemetry keeps
scoring, not a leap or an adaptive scheme.

## Scope

In scope:

- `minStartGapMs` 1,000 to 250 and the policy stamp to `serial-250ms-v1`;
  the stamp travels through the existing `policy` and `policy_min_gap_ms`
  report fields with no schema change.
- Update the harness assertions that pin the old gap and stamp
  (inter-start spacing, `scheduled_wait_ms`, and the policy fields in the
  listing and shuffle report cases). This assertion change is the planned
  behavior change, not absorption.
- Update the integration page's pacing sentence and recorded policy name.

Out of scope:

- Any further gap reduction. A second step waits for post-change telemetry
  showing the new pace clean under real use.
- Reintroducing request concurrency or any adaptive/dynamic pacing; the
  serial lane's simplicity and write-ordering guarantee stay.
- The liked-tracks quota 429s. They are pressure-independent; the levers
  are the Spotify dashboard's extension request or support, not pacing.
- Server or telemetry schema changes; none are needed.

## Design

**One constant, one stamp, same lane.** The gap and the stamp are the only
behavior changes. The cooldown persistence, single bounded 429 retry,
cancellation, and countdown rendering are untouched safety nets; if the
faster pace ever draws a pressure-correlated 429, the lane already backs
off, and the report rows carrying `serial-250ms-v1` make the ceiling
measurable instead of guessed.

**Why 250 ms.** Four requests per second raises the peak rolling window
from 30 to roughly 120 per 30 seconds. The pre-telemetry code ran
six-concurrent bursts of ~90-100 requests repeatedly without a single 429
on these endpoints, so this step stays inside behavior Spotify has already
tolerated while remaining a single bounded move. Gap waits of 250 ms stay
below the wait-notice threshold, so no countdown or UI change is involved.

**Evidence attribution is the rollback story.** Every report row names its
policy. If `serial-250ms-v1` rows accumulate pressure-correlated 429s, the
constant reverts in one commit and the comparison between stamps is a
telemetry query; that is the entire governance loop this repository's
telemetry was built for.

## Affected Components

- `web/app.js`: `minStartGapMs` and the `telemetryPolicy` stamp.
- `web/app_test.js`: the spacing, `scheduled_wait_ms`, and policy-field
  assertions move to the new values; the fake-clock advances are already
  multiples of the new gap and stay unchanged.
- `doc/architecture/integration/SPOTIFY_INTEGRATION.md`: the pacing
  description and the recorded policy name.

## Implementation Sequence

Single-step change: constant, stamp, assertions, and the doc sentence in
one commit; validate, push, deploy per the standing directive through the
private runbook.


The harness must prove request starts remain strictly serialized with at
least 250 ms spacing and that reports carry `serial-250ms-v1` with
`policy_min_gap_ms` 250. After deployment and real use, the telemetry
review -- refusals by policy stamp and rolling-window pressure at any
failure -- is the checkpoint that closes this step and gates any next one.

## Success Criteria

- A cache-hit shuffle of the 4,549-track playlist completes in roughly 15
  seconds instead of ~47, with request starts serialized at >= 250 ms.
- New telemetry rows carry `serial-250ms-v1` / 250 / retry ceiling 1;
  older rows remain attributed to `serial-1000ms-v1`.
- No behavior change in cooldowns, retries, cancellation, ordering, or UI.
- The purity grep stays clean; all prior tests pass unmodified except the
  pacing and policy assertions named in scope.
